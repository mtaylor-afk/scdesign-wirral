Is it time?

Signs your roof needs replacing.

A few slipped tiles can usually be repaired. When problems keep coming back, a new roof is often the better investment.

  • Slipping slates

    On older slate roofs the nails rust through ("nail fatigue"), so slates keep sliding out however often they're refixed.

  • Damp and leaks

    Damp patches in the loft or bedroom ceilings, especially after heavy rain.

  • A sagging roof line

    A dipping ridge or a wavy roof line can point to tired battens or timbers underneath.

  • Crumbling felt

    Brittle, torn or missing underlay, visible from inside the loft.

  • Porous, cracked tiles

    Flaking or heavily mossed tiles hold water, then crack when it freezes.

  • Age

    Many concrete-tiled roofs across Merseyside are now 50 to 70 years old.

What's included

Everything in a complete re-roof.

Every quote is itemised. A typical WV Roofing replacement covers all of this.

  • Scaffolding, edge protection, and covers for your garden and paths
  • Stripping the old tiles or slates, battens and felt
  • Checking the rafters, and replacing damaged timber (priced separately if needed)
  • A breathable roofing membrane and new treated battens
  • Your chosen tiles or slates, fixed to current standards
  • A mechanically fixed dry ridge and dry verge, with no mortar to crumble
  • New leadwork and flashings around chimneys and abutments
  • Roof ventilation to help prevent condensation in the loft
  • Removing all waste, and a final walk-round with you

Doing the fascias at the same time?

With the scaffold already up, it's the cheapest time to swap old timber fascias, soffits and gutters for low-maintenance uPVC. Ask for it as an option on your quote.

Choosing your roof

Which roof is right for you?

Slate suits most Victorian and Edwardian homes, and concrete tiles are the great-value choice for most post-war houses. Clay tiles and Welsh slate are the heritage options.

Regulations & paperwork

Planning, Building Regulations and your roof.

Planning permission. Replacing a roof like-for-like doesn't usually need planning permission in England. Homes in conservation areas, listed buildings and properties covered by an Article 4 direction are different, and a noticeably different material can need consent. Wirral and Liverpool both have many conservation areas, so we check before any work starts.

Building Regulations. A full roof replacement normally needs Building Regulations sign-off, because it counts as renovating a "thermal element". That can mean upgrading the loft or rafter insulation at the same time. We'll explain what applies to your roof and how the sign-off is handled.

This is general guidance for England, not legal advice. Always confirm with your local authority if you're unsure.
