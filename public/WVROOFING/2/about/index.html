What this is

WV Roofing is a hobby concept for a roof-replacement business on the Wirral and in Liverpool. It isn't trading. There are no prices, reviews, guarantees or accreditations here, because there's no business behind them yet, and nothing on this site is an offer.

This is the second version: a weather report. The first version is a more conventional site with the original visualiser.

The first version's visualiser works differently: it uploads your photo to private storage to make its pictures, and it has its own privacy page. Everything below is about this version.

Your photo stays on your device

The Roof Cam works entirely in your browser. When you take or choose a photo:

  • Your browser opens it on your own device, turns it the right way up and makes a working copy.
  • Finding the roof, drawing the eight roofs and the weather all happen on that copy, on your device.
  • Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere. There's no account, no address and no server involved.
  • When you close the tab, the photo is gone. The share card and saved image only leave your device if you share or send them yourself.

You don't have to take our word for it. After you add a photo, the Roof Cam counts every request its page makes and names where they went (a "network light" under the controls). Normally it stays at zero.

What this site does store

One thing: if you press Weather in the bar at the top to switch the moving weather off (or back on), your browser remembers that choice on this device, so the next page stays the way you left it. If your choice is what your device already asks for (reduced motion, or saving data), nothing is stored at all. It's a single setting, not a cookie, and it's never sent anywhere.

What your browser fetches

  • The pages, their stylesheets, scripts and icon, two small data files (the roof catalogue and the list of sample houses), and the three sample photographs with small previews of them, all from this site.
  • The typefaces (Big Shoulders Display, Archivo and IBM Plex Mono), from Google Fonts. Your browser connects to Google to download them, as it would for many websites.

There are no analytics, advertising or tracking of any kind, and no cookies, so there's no cookie banner. Cloudflare hosts the site.

How the roofs are drawn

Each of the eight roofs is described by a handful of numbers: its two colours, the size and shape of its tiles or slates, how the courses overlap. The Roof Cam lays those courses across the roof you marked, parallel to the eaves, and keeps your photo's own light and shadow, so the new roof sits in the same daylight as the house.

You tap each slope of the roof. From each tap the Roof Cam grows a region out from where you tapped, following the roof's colour and stopping at strong edges. It's quick and usually close; you can tighten or loosen it, cut out chimneys, or draw the outline yourself.

The weather is a colour grade and some rain. Wet roofs are darkened and given a little sheen. None of it is a forecast, and all of it is approximate: screens show colour differently, and nothing replaces holding a real tile or slate.

Credits

The sample houses are stock photographs, with number plates and house numbers blurred: the 1930s semi and the 1920s-30s detached house are from Unsplash (Unsplash Licence), and the modern detached house is from Pexels (Pexels Licence).

No manufacturer's pictures are used. Where a maker's product is named, it's only to say what a finish is comparable to.
