What this is, what happens to your photo, and how the roofs are drawn.

What this is

WV Roofing is a hobby concept for a roof-replacement business on the Wirral and in Liverpool. It isn't trading. There are no prices, reviews, guarantees or accreditations here, because there's no business behind them yet, and nothing on this site is an offer.

This is the second version: a weather report. The first version is a more conventional site with the original visualiser.

Both versions keep customers' photos and enquiries in the same private store, and WV Roofing's privacy notice covers them both. Here's what it means for this one.

What happens to your photo

When you take or choose a photo in the Roof Cam:

  • The roofs are drawn on your device. Your browser opens the photo, turns it the right way up, and finds the roof and draws the eight roofs and the weather on it, there and then. No AI service sees it.
  • It's saved for the roofer. At the same time the photo is uploaded to WV Roofing's private store, so the roofer can see the house you mean. Location data, camera details and other hidden information in the file are removed when it arrives; the picture itself isn't changed. (A photo the store can't take as it is, such as a very large one, or one sent over a slow connection, is sent as a smaller JPEG made on your device.) Only the WV Roofing team can open it, and your browser holds a key to it for this tab only.
  • Kept for 30 days. It's deleted automatically after 30 days, unless you send an enquiry about it: then it's kept with your enquiry for 12 months.
  • Delete it whenever you like. While your photo is on screen, a light under the Roof Cam's controls shows when it's saved, with a button to delete it straight away (if you've sent it to the roofer, it's taken out of your enquiry too). After that, ask the roofer to delete it.
  • If saving isn't switched on (WV Roofing is a concept, so it may not be), the light says so, and your photo stays on your device. The roofs work just the same.
  • The sample houses are never saved: they aren't yours. The share card and saved image only leave your device if you share or send them yourself.

If you send it to the roofer

"Send it to the roofer" sends your name, a phone number or email address, your postcode if you give it, your message and the roof you chose. If you leave the box ticked, your photo and a preview of that roof go with it. The bulletin's form at the bottom of the first page sends the same details without a photo. Either way it's saved in WV Roofing's private database first, with a reference, and then the roofer is told by email. It's used only to reply to you and prepare a quote (taking steps you asked for before a contract), and it's kept for 12 months, then deleted automatically.

What this site stores in your browser

  • The key to your saved photo, for this tab only. It disappears when you close the tab.
  • If you press Weather in the bar at the top to switch the moving weather off (or back on), your browser remembers that choice on this device, so the next page stays the way you left it. If your choice is what your device already asks for (reduced motion, or saving data), nothing is stored. It's a single setting, not a cookie, and it's never sent anywhere.

Who's involved

  • Cloudflare hosts the pages, the roof catalogue and the sample photographs.
  • Vercel runs WV Roofing's server and stores your photo privately (Vercel Blob, London). Neon holds the record of it and any enquiry (a database in London). Apple iCloud Mail delivers the email to the roofer.
  • Google Fonts serves the typefaces (Big Shoulders Display, Archivo and IBM Plex Mono), so your browser connects to Google to download them, as it would for many websites.

There are no analytics, advertising or tracking of any kind, and no cookies, so there's no cookie banner. Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of your data, or for it to be corrected or deleted, and you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

How the roofs are drawn

Each of the eight roofs is described by a handful of numbers: its two colours, the size and shape of its tiles or slates, how the courses overlap. The Roof Cam lays those courses across the roof you marked, parallel to the eaves, and keeps your photo's own light and shadow, so the new roof sits in the same daylight as the house.

You tap each slope of the roof. From each tap the Roof Cam grows a region out from where you tapped, following the roof's colour and stopping at strong edges. It's quick and usually close; you can tighten or loosen it, cut out chimneys, or draw the outline yourself.

The weather is a colour grade and some rain. Wet roofs are darkened and given a little sheen. None of it is a forecast, and all of it is approximate: screens show colour differently, and nothing replaces holding a real tile or slate.
