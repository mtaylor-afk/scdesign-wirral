Front 04 · Under the tiles

What a new roof involves.

A re-roof is more than the part you can see from the street. In order, from the scaffold to the skip:

  1. Scaffold

    Scaffolding goes up, and the garden, paths and windows below are covered.

  2. Strip

    The old tiles or slates come off, then the battens and the old felt under them.

  3. Timbers

    With the roof open, the rafters and the other timbers can be checked properly.

  4. Membrane

    A breathable membrane goes over the rafters: the roof's second line of defence.

  5. Battens

    New treated battens are fixed at the spacing the chosen tile or slate needs.

  6. Tiles or slates

    The new covering is laid course by course, from the eaves up to the ridge.

  7. Ridge and lead

    Ridge tiles and verges are fixed, and new lead is dressed where the roof meets chimneys and walls.

  8. Tidy

    The scaffold comes down and the waste goes away. The rain can do what it likes.

A general description of the trade. Not a programme, a price or a promise.

Front 06 · Viewers' questions

You asked.

What happens to my photo?

Nothing. Your browser opens it on your own device and it stays there. It isn't uploaded, stored or sent to anyone, and it's gone when you close the tab. It only leaves your device if you share the card or send a saved image yourself.

You don't have to take that on trust: after you add a photo, the Roof Cam counts every request its own page makes from then on (normally none) and shows you the number.

How accurate are the previews?

They're a way to narrow down a choice, not a photograph of a finished roof. Each roof is drawn from numbers on your device, keeping your photo's own light and shadow, and it's approximate.

Screens show colour differently too. Nothing replaces holding a real tile or slate in your hand.

Can I try it without a photo?

Yes. The Roof Cam has three sample houses with the roof already marked: a 1930s semi, a modern detached house and a 1920s-30s detached house. They're stock photographs, with number plates and house numbers blurred.

Do I need planning permission for a new roof?

Usually not for a like-for-like replacement in England. If your home is in a conservation area or is a listed building, or you'd like a noticeably different material, you may need consent.

This is general guidance for England, not legal advice. Always confirm with your local authority if you're unsure.

Does a new roof need Building Regulations approval?

A full re-roof normally does. It counts as renovating a "thermal element", which can mean upgrading the roof insulation at the same time.

This is general guidance for England, not legal advice. Always confirm with your local authority if you're unsure.

Is the weather real?

No. The rain, the wind, the lightning and the forecast are all theatre, drawn on your device. If the moving weather is too much, press Weather in the bar at the top to still it, and press it again to bring it back. It starts still if your device asks for reduced motion or is saving data, unless you've switched the weather back on here.
