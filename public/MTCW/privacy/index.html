What we collect.

There are two ways to contact us through this website: a quick message on the contact page, and a more detailed website brief for when you are ready to tell us about your new website. We collect what you enter in them from you directly.

The quick message asks for:

  • your name
  • your email address
  • your business area (Wirral, Liverpool, or both)
  • your message about what your business does and what you would like from the website

You can also choose to add:

  • a phone number
  • your business name
  • what you are interested in (the website, monthly changes, both, or not sure yet)
  • your town or district
  • an existing website or social media page
  • whether you would prefer a video call or an in-person planning meeting

The website brief asks for:

  • your name and an email address for Matt's reply
  • your business name
  • at least one service or product you offer
  • where you serve your customers
  • the main thing you want visitors to your website to do ("Help me choose" is a valid answer)

It asks for a phone number only if you would like Matt to reply by phone. Everything else in the brief is optional: how you would prefer Matt to reply and to hold the planning meeting; more about your business, your customers and why they choose you; what you would like your website to show, such as contact details, an address, opening hours, prices, reviews or qualifications, common questions, extra features and a style; existing websites or social pages, and similar businesses' websites with what they offer that you do not; a sharing link for larger files; and anything else you want Matt to know.

Your reply details (your name, email address, phone number and reply and meeting preferences) are private. They are for Matt to contact you, and they are kept apart from any contact details you choose to show on your website.

With a website brief you can also send up to 8 photos or files: JPEG, PNG, WebP or HEIC photos, or PDF documents, each up to 3 MB as sent. For each one you can say what it is, how it should be used, and anything else about it. If you send photos, files, review wording or similar material, the form asks you to confirm that you have permission to share it for preparing your website.

JPEG, PNG and WebP photos larger than 3 MB are made smaller in your own browser before they are sent, which also removes details stored inside the photo, such as where it was taken. Photos of 3 MB or less are sent exactly as they are, so any details your phone or camera stored inside them, which can include the location, are sent too.

Please send only material you are allowed to share, and do not include passwords, payment details or private information about your customers.

If you reached a form from a link on this website, the form may also send which kind of page the link was on and any business area or interest that link chose, from a short fixed list. No other tracking information is sent.

To limit spam and repeated sending, each form includes a hidden field that people do not normally see. The service that receives the forms also keeps short-lived counts of recent submissions, and a note of each form it has just sent so that a repeated send is not delivered twice. These are held only in its working memory and are never saved anywhere; each is used for no more than 15 minutes and is cleared out after that. The counts are filed under a one-way coded value made from your network (IP) address, not the address itself, and neither contains your name, email address or message.

How your form reaches us.

Neither form saves your answers part-way. What you type stays only in the open page until you send it, so if you close the page first, it is not kept anywhere.

When you send a form, your browser sends it over an encrypted connection to a small service that runs on Vercel, in the Vercel project Matt also uses for the SC Design Wirral website (scdesign-wirral). The service checks what you sent and emails it to Matt's iCloud mailbox through iCloud Mail. Your email address is set as the reply address, so Matt's reply comes straight to you. The form does not send you an email itself.

A quick message arrives as one email. A website brief arrives as one email with your answers and three attachments: a readable copy of your answers, a separate file with your private reply details, and a "build pack", a zip file that arranges your answers for preparing a website and leaves out your private reply details. Each photo or file you add then follows in its own separate email.

MT Creative Works keeps no separate copy of your form in a database: Matt's mailbox is the record of what you sent. The service that sends the emails holds your answers and files only while it checks and sends them.

If an email cannot be sent, the form tells you and does not show it as sent. You can try again, or send a photo or file by replying to Matt's email instead.

Why we use it.

We use what you send to reply to you and to understand what you need. With a website brief, Matt also uses your answers, photos and files to plan your website with you and to prepare a first draft of it. Client information supports the work we agree to carry out. Necessary technical information helps us operate the website and its forms and protect them from misuse.

When Matt prepares a draft website from a brief, he may use website-building software, including Claude Code, an AI coding tool made by Anthropic, with the build pack and the photos and files you sent. The build pack leaves out your private reply details. Sending a form does not start any AI tool: Matt decides whether to use one after reading your brief. What you need to provide.

For a quick message we need your name, email address, business area and message. For a website brief we need your name, email address, business name, at least one service or product, where you serve your customers and the main thing you want visitors to do, plus a phone number if you ask for a reply by phone. Without these we cannot understand your enquiry or reply to it. Everything else, including photos and files, is optional.

Neither form asks for payment details, a budget or an account, and you do not need to agree to marketing to send either one. Sending a form does not place an order or take payment.
